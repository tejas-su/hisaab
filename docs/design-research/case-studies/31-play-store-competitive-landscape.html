Where hisaab stands on the Play Store

A look at the expense trackers an Indian Android user would compare us with: what they do, what we do that they don't, what we lack, and what's worth building without breaking the "5-year-old can use it" bar.

The headline
No popular India-first Android app answers “how much can I spend today?”

The apps that do are US-only and bank-linked (PocketGuard), abandoned (Daily Budget, last updated 2019) or iOS-only (Today's Budget).

Our biggest gaps
  • Every spend is typed by hand
  • No widget or quick tile
  • English only
  • Nothing brings people back to log

Five camps

Manual ledgers

Money Manager, Monefy, Money Lover

Huge user base; accounts, calendars, charts. None answers “how much today?”. Reviews complain about ads, moves to subscriptions and recurring entries that are hard to edit. One Money Manager reviewer asks outright for a “safe to spend” figure.

Feature-rich hybrids

Wallet, Spendee, Cashew

Multiple accounts and currencies, sync, widgets, goals, CSV in and out. They win on depth and pay with a learning curve. Cashew shows a local-first app with no sign-in can earn 4.9★.

SMS auto-capture

axio, PennyWise, FinArt, Kharch Log

They remove typing, the #1 reason people quit. Reliability is the weak spot: axio's 2026 reviews describe missed banks, duplicates, and an app that won't open. Play needs a separate SMS declaration.

Account Aggregator

Fold, CRED Money

Your bank data, imported with consent. Needs a network and an account; works only for banks that have joined.

Daily-number apps

PocketGuard, Daily Budget, Today's Budget, Plan & Multiply

Reviewers say the one number “changes behaviour on day one”. This is hisaab's camp, and in India it's empty.

What to build

Each item is weighed on how much users want it against how well it fits hisaab's rules: zero setup, 4-tap entry, no internet, one signal colour.

Do nextHigh demand, good fit
1

Home-screen widget

Shows “₹1,240 left today” and opens the Add tray at the keypad.

The core value becomes glanceable, and every entry loses a step. Needs home_widget (already in PLAN §11). Open question: should it show the amount by default?

2

Quick Settings tile

“Add a spend” from the pull-down shade.

Tiny to build; saves taps for heavy users. Already in PLAN §11.

3

Hindi

A full Hindi version of the app.

Best match for our audience. Our fonts already have Devanagari. The copy needs native review, especially the hero sentence and plurals.

4

Evening nudge, opt-in

About 9 pm, only on a day with no entries: “Anything to add for today?” Opens on the keypad.

Reuses the bill-reminder plumbing. Asked once. No streaks, no guilt.

Look into firstHigh demand, risky fit
5

Spends from payment notifications

When GPay, PhonePe or a bank app says “Paid ₹240”, hisaab offers a one-tap confirm with the amount filled in. You still pick the tile.

Avoids SMS: Play approval is hard, and SMS readers miss and duplicate spends, including card bills we're careful not to count twice. Needs a research spike and a prototype, not a build.

6

Voice entry

“Chai 40.”

Indian rivals push Hinglish voice but send audio to a server. Android's offline recogniser could keep us off the internet; Hinglish quality unknown. Test with 5 users.

7

Phone-lock unlock

Open hisaab with the phone's own fingerprint or PIN.

Little work, but it's a setting. One row in Your hisaab, or test whether hiding amounts in recent apps already covers what people fear.

LaterMedium demand, good fit
8

CSV import

From Money Manager, Monefy and Wallet exports, offered next to Restore on the first onboarding screen.

9

CSV export

As M6 plans, reusing the Ask AI CSV writer.

For the store listing

Screenshot 1 is today's number, not a chart.
Every big rival leads with a chart or a list.
“No sign-in. No bank login. No internet. No ads.”
True, and checkable on the Data safety form; some rivals' “on device” copy contradicts their own policy.
Name the Indian details.
Payday on any date, rent and EMI out before your daily share, card bills not counted twice.

Decisions for you

Will hisaab ever make money? If so, “no ads” can't be promised for good.
Is Hindi in scope for the first release?
Should the widget show today's amount by default? Same privacy question as hiding amounts in recent apps.
Is reading payment notifications on the phone acceptable in principle?
