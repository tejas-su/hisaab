Changing an entry, round 2

Round 1 (mockup 47) started every version from the Add tray or a form, so changing an entry still felt like filling it in again. Round 2 started from the entry instead: see it first, then tap the part that's wrong. Every phone below works. Tap the Food ₹250 row and try fixing the amount, moving it to Travel, or changing the note. Everything saves the moment you pick, with Undo, so there's no Save and no Next.

What the research says

  1. Tapping shows the thing first. Things 3 opens a task as a card in place, "while the rest of the list fades". Copilot, Monzo, Splitwise and Money Manager open a details view. None of them reopen the add flow.
  2. The value is the button. Copilot: "tap on the current date or name to edit them". Monzo: "tap the category shown at the top". There's no Edit mode and no form.
  3. Never make people re-enter what didn't change. A Monefy user: when editing, the accounts "are reset and have to manually specify them… difficult when I can't recall". That's exactly your report.
  4. Undo beats Save, Discard and confirmations for changes that can be reversed (NN/g; About Face). Things saves as you type.
  5. Stay where you were. Monzo users complain the app jumps to the home page after an edit. Monzo users also ask for quick actions right under a row, so they don't have to open another screen.
  6. Adding and changing are different jobs. Splitwise redesigns, form tools and our own bill sheet (D118) all split them.

A · The row opens up where it is like Things 3

Tap the row and it grows into a card right there in the list, with everything else faded. The amount, category, day and note are each a button. Pick something and it's saved straight away, and the card says so. Done folds it back, and one Undo reverses everything you changed.

  1. Amount: row → ₹250 → digits → Save → Done
  2. Category: row → Food ▾ → Travel → Done
  3. Note: row → tap the note → type → Done (no sheet; typed right in the card)

Round 2b (owner): the note is edited in place, and Food ▾ / Today ▾ carry a caret, so they read as buttons, not labels. Until the first change, the card says "Tap any part to change it".

Feels like: touching the entry itself. You never leave the list. Risk: a card inside a scrolling list is fiddly at large text sizes and in landscape.
