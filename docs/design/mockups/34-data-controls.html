Your hisaab: Back up, Restore, Delete everything proposal

SPEC BR-16, FR-SE-4, FR-ON-1 and E14, drawn from the product brief, the UX flows and copy deck 34 (every string is verbatim), with research in case study 23. Nothing here is built. A new Settings group, Your hisaab, sits under Look. Card 1 holds Back up and Restore, plus Undo restore for 7 days after a restore. Card 2 holds Delete everything on its own, 24dp away, so a slip off Restore can't land on it.

Today is Fri 6 Nov 2026, with ₹45,000 coming in on the 4th. The phone has 37 entries and no backup yet. The old phone's file, hisaab-backup-2026-10-12.json, was made on 12 Oct and holds 214 entries. Today's backup is hisaab-backup-2026-11-06.json. Frames are 360dp; the last section has 320dp frames at 2× text. Use the switches to change theme or show the 20, 24 and 74dp edges.

The hold key without red

The hold key is the only strong action on the delete page, and the only key in the app that can't be undone. Four ways to make it strong were compared, in both themes. Try each one: press and hold for 2 seconds, or let go early.

Why
Not marigoldMarigold means today / act. It is Add, Next and Save: the everyday "go ahead" key the hand already trusts. On a page meant to make you stop, the brightest colour in the app would pull the eye and thumb to the one act that can't be undone. It would also make Delete look like Save. That breaks the brief ("the hold key is never marigold") and the colour's job. The rule drawn here: marigold never goes on an act that removes data. So the restore dialog's Replace isn't marigold either.
Not an ink slabA black key at rest is the "black slab" the colour process rules out (D105). An ink fill also already means picked (D100), and a filled key at rest leaves nothing for the progress to fill.
Not a hatchHatching already means over your share / over the limit (§3.3, Spends). Using it here would blur a money state with a danger state. It also reads as "disabled" on a pale page.
Chosen: outline, then fillAt rest, a 2dp inverse edge and an ink label with the Delete02 glyph. Every other key in the app is fill-only (D88, D100), so this is the only outlined key: different in kind, not louder. While held, an inverse fill sweeps start → end, linear over 2.0 s, so its length tells the time. The label flips to bg under the fill, so every letter stays readable at every point. When full, the key is one solid inverse block reading "Let go to delete". The caption "Keep your finger on it for 2 seconds" is always shown, so it works with reduced motion and for people who can't see the fill. The words carry the meaning; the fill only shows progress.
MeasuredEdge and fill inverse on bg: 11.42 light / 16.55 dark (≥ 3:1 for UI parts). Label under the fill, bg on inverse: 11.42 / 16.55. Resting label ink on bg: 15.30 / 16.55. Caption ink2 on bg: 6.14 / 7.31. OKLCH L: inverse 0.303 light (a charcoal; ink is 0.200) and 0.958 dark (a light pill on the 0.183 page, the snackbar's step). Back up first beside it is control on bg, 1.17 / 1.56, with an ink label at 17.97 / 10.59, so the squint test reads the hold key first. No new colour: it reuses inverse, the token for a dark fill.

For you to decide

  • Hairline indent. Drawn at 74dp, the text start (the pixel rule and mockups 32 and 33). The build's settings_page.dart indents the Money dividers by gutter (20dp). One should change, and this group should follow whichever wins.
  • The onboarding chip costs a 48dp row. The progress dots are centred, and at 320dp a 150dp chip can't share their row without overlapping them. So the chip gets its own row above them on step 1 only. Step 1 still fits a 760dp phone. On smallPhone, step 1 already scrolls.
  • A snackbar on the delete page covers the hold key for its 4 s after Back up first (frame D2). That's a short pause, which is fine. The alternative is to show no snackbar here and let the updated "Backed up today" line say it, but then the file name is lost.
  • Back up first stays quiet. It is a control key, not a strong one. If the hallway test shows people skip it, raise it to a sunk well with the last-backup line inside, still never marigold.
