Home-screen widget and Quick Settings tile

Two ways to reach hisaab without opening it. The widget is for glancing: today's safe-to-spend, without a tap. The tile is for entering: one swipe down, then the keypad. Both open the Add tray at the keypad, so the path stays Add → digits → Next → tile; they only skip finding and opening the app.

Numbers follow the spec's worked example: today's share ₹1,850, chai ₹40 then lunch ₹380, ₹1,430 left, 9 days to payday on 4 Nov.

2 · The same card as Home

The widget is cut from the hero's paper. It uses the same finish (D106, D107), drawn from the numbers in DisplayEtchPainter:

  • the guilloché rosette, 14 rippled rings centred 80dp in from the top end corner
  • the fixed-seed grain, one dot per 60 sq dp
  • the solid keyline in displayEtch, 6dp inside the edge
  • the dashed hairline keyline 10dp inside, with 4dp dashes and 3dp gaps
  • the displayEtch divider above the range line

On a 360dp phone, Home's hero and the 4×2 widget are both 320dp wide, so the rosette and frame land in the same place on both.

3 · Four sizes, one widget

One widget that resizes (Android 12+ responsive layouts), so there's only one entry in the launcher's widget list for this, not four. Each size keeps the hero's order: the amount first, then today's bar, then the days. Every size has the marigold Add key at the same corner: 48dp, or a 40dp key in a 48dp tap target on the 2×1. Every size carries the hero's rosette, grain and double keyline.

What the widget leaves out

  • The bar's captions ("₹420 spent", "of ₹1,850 for today") and the category names under bites. TalkBack still reads them.
  • The This week view. A widget can't be toggled without a tap that also opens something; a tap opens Home instead.
  • Motion. The bar doesn't drain on the widget; it redraws when you come back to the home screen.
  • Room around the frame. The hero keeps 24dp above and below its content; the widget keeps the 10dp gap inside the dashed line and no more, because 84dp and 184dp are fixed by the launcher's grid.

Private, as a second widget

Switch Amount → Private above. The amount gives way to words ("Plenty left today", "A little left today", "Over today's share") and the bar stays, so a glance still says how today is going without a rupee figure for others to read.

Proposed as a separate entry in the widget list (hisaab and hisaab · private), so the choice is made by picking one, not by a setting (PLAN §2).

5 · Quick Settings tile

The tile does one thing: Add a spend. It is an action tile, so it never shows as "on" and uses the system's colours (Android draws tiles, not the app). The icon is a monochrome Hugeicons glyph; the label is the verb.

Tile behaviour

  1. Tap: the shade collapses and the Add tray opens at the keypad.
  2. Tapped while locked: Android asks to unlock first (unlockAndRun), then step 1. Nothing about money shows before unlocking.
  3. Long-press: opens hisaab's Home (the tile's preferences activity).
  4. TalkBack: "Add a spend, hisaab. Button."

Also free: an app shortcut

Long-press the launcher icon → Add a spend. A static shortcut with no UI to design and no settings; it opens the same tray at the keypad.

6 · How people add them

Most people never open the widget list or edit their tiles. Android lets the app ask: requestPinAppWidget for the widget and requestAddTileService (Android 13+) for the tile, each answered in a system dialog. Proposed: one Settings group, Shortcuts, with a row for each. A row hides itself when the launcher or Android version can't do it.

9 · Build notes and open points

Never stale at midnight

A widget lives when the app is closed, but all money maths stays in Dart (SafeToSpend, PeriodCalculator). Proposal: each time the app saves anything, it writes a day-by-day table until the next payday and one cycle past it: hero, share, state, days left, range line. Kotlin only picks today's row. This works because nothing changes the ledger without the app being open: with no new entries, every future day's figure is already known, bills and payday included.

Refresh: on every save, delete, undo, restore and settings change; a widget update at about 00:01 and on date, time or time-zone change. Past the table's end (months unopened) the widget shows the wordmark and "Open hisaab to update".

Drawing it

  • home_widget is already named in PLAN §11. Layouts are native RemoteViews XML; Android 12+ size mapping gives the four sizes from one provider.
  • To verify on device: whether RemoteViews keeps bundled Anek Latin with tabular figures (an XML fontFamily from res/font). If not, the amount is drawn as a bitmap.
  • Today's bar with bites: a bitmap from home_widget's render-a-Flutter-widget call, reusing the real painter; the table's future rows draw a plain full bar natively.
  • The look follows the app's Look setting, not just the phone's, so the app writes its theme into the snapshot.
  • Corners follow system_app_widget_background_radius on Android 12+.

The tile

  • A plain Kotlin TileService; no Flutter package needed (quick_settings_tile in PLAN §11 is optional).
  • Opens with startActivityAndCollapse(PendingIntent) on Android 14+, the Intent form below it; unlockAndRun when locked.
  • The app routes an add launch straight to the tray's keypad, the same route the widget and the app shortcut use.

Docs this would change

  • SPEC: new FR for the widget and tile (states, taps, refresh), and FR-SE for the Shortcuts rows.
  • UI_DESIGN §10: replace "Later quick-add options" with this; NFR-10 gains the private widget and the tile's no-money rule.
