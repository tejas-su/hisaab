Home, Next 7 days: direction B, a week rail

Seven calm day cells, tomorrow to day 7. A day with bills shows an icon (or a count) and its amount; empty days stay quiet outlines. One sunk line below says what goes out. Tap a day to open its bills in place. Today in these frames is Mon 26 Oct, as in 02-home. Marigold is not used on the strip (the hero keeps its today block). State is outline, dash, hatch and words, never colour alone.

Alignment and spacing

  • Strip card has the hero card's outer edges: 20dp from the screen (HisaabSpacing.gutter), 28dp radius, 12dp below the line above.
  • Title and summary text start 20dp in: card padding 4 plus 16 inner, the same inset as the hero's text. Expansion rows use the 16dp row inset and the hairline starts at the text start (74dp from the card edge), as in the Today list.
  • The rail's hit areas fill the card's 342dp inner width at 48.9dp each. Visuals are inset 2dp, giving 4dp between boxes. Gaps and radii use the 4/8dp scale (14dp radius as the icon wells).
  • Frame 3 draws the 20dp gutter as dashed lines. This is my reading of the CSS, not a measured screenshot; check the real edges in a widget test (rows_share_the_hero_cards_edges).

Tap targets

  • Day with bills: at least 48.9 × 82dp hit area; the whole cell is the button.
  • Empty day: not a button, no action. It is an outline so the week still reads as seven days.
  • Expansion row: full card width, 56dp high, opens that bill's sheet (existing onOpenBill).
  • Cell minimum = max(48dp, measured amount width). At 1x on a 390dp phone that is 48.9dp, so seven fit. Narrower or larger text wraps the rail (frame 5). It never shrinks below 48dp or clips.

TalkBack

  • Group: "Next 7 days. 13,200 rupees goes out. Electricity next, Wednesday." The warning, when shown, is read straight after the title.
  • Day cell: "Wednesday 28 October: Electricity, about 1,200 rupees. Opens the bills for this day." (The brief's example said Thursday 26 October, but 26 Oct is a Monday in these frames, so the example is moved to Wed 28.)
  • Card day: "Thursday 29 October: Credit card, 8,400 rupees, pay by Thursday, not counted. Opens the bills for this day." A counted day under warning adds "Uses today's share."
  • Empty days are excluded from semantics. The open day reports expanded, and the panel is a labelled region. Focus moves to the first row on open and back to the cell on close.
  • The letter T and S are ambiguous; the label always carries the full weekday and date, so the letter is visual only.

Against a plain list (direction A style)

Pros

  • Shows when, not just what: clusters (rent plus card on one weekend) are visible at a glance.
  • Quiet when the week is empty or light: six outlines cost less attention than rows.
  • Short by default: about 170dp for the rail and 56dp for the line, however many bills there are, until a day is opened.

Cons

  • Names are hidden until a tap (or the soonest named in the line). A list shows every name now.
  • An extra tap for the second bill on a day, and a count instead of an icon loses what the bills are.
  • Weekday letters are cryptic (T, S twice). Dates carry the meaning.
  • Large text or narrow windows break the seven-in-a-row shape; the wrapped rail reads more like a calendar grid than a timeline.
  • Cell amounts at 12sp are small, and "about" cannot fit in a cell; it lives in the line and the expansion.

Open questions for the owner

  1. A day with 2 or more bills: show the sum (as here) or only the count? The sum includes the card bill if there is one, which could look like it is counted.
  2. When text is large, is a wrapped rail acceptable, or should the strip fall back to a plain list below a certain scale?
  3. A day with exactly one bill: expand inline anyway (consistent, one tap) or open the bill sheet directly (faster, but the same cell then acts two ways)?
