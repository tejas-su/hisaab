6. Caveats from the case study

  • B's bg #EEECE7 is close to the pre-D108 page #EEECE6 that the owner called "unpainted". B is a return to a value already seen, with a graded step beneath it. It only helps if the problem is the page reading beige, not dull.
  • B mixes hues. bg sits at OKLCH hue 89 and ink2 at 85, but sunk and hairline fall back to about 72. A final B should pick one hue. B also supersedes D108's "hue about 75°, warm toward marigold" rule.
  • C's ink2 drifts to hue 54, toward brown-red, and should be re-solved near 72 before any trial. C is also the "stronger tint" D108 rejected, and its page is stronger than any sourced warm product (Jupiter's beige drew a "dull" comment).
  • D changes only the page. bg #F5F1EC is CRED's page: lighter (L 0.960 against 0.943) and fainter (chroma 0.008), still at hue 74, so D108's hue rule holds. Every other token is today's. The cost: cards, chips and the Back well on the page lift 1.12:1 instead of 1.17, about a third less, and outdoors the step is near case study 29's glare margin (about 1.06 at F 1.0). The hero etch, drawn in bg, gets fainter. Marigold on bg is 1.63:1, so it keeps its ink edge.
  • Same for all: every contrast floor passes on A, B, C and D. On A, B and C marigold on bg stays 1.55:1, so the ink edge is permanent. Not measured: APCA, and anything on a device. Hex values are hand-built starting points to be re-solved with the better-colors skill.
